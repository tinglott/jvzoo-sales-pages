What research actually says about supplements — checked by a nurse

Supplement marketing runs on hope. This reference runs on evidence.

The Supplement Evidence Guide is a reference app — the guide itself does not sell supplements. Included in this package is a separate Supplement Business Starter directory for buyers interested in starting their own supplement business: affiliate programs, private-label suppliers, and wholesale/dropship contacts. Every entry is labeled — affiliate link or plain contact — and the full disclosure is printed at the top of the directory. It covers 23 health conditions — sleep, blood sugar support, joint pain, heart and metabolic health, gut health, memory, strength, and more. Every condition card follows the same honest structure:

  • What it is — the supplements most commonly studied for the condition
  • What research shows — a plain-language summary with an evidence grade: Strong, Moderate, Mixed, or Limited. When the evidence is weak, the card says so.
  • Cautions & interactions — visually separate, impossible to miss: prescription interactions, kidney/liver cautions, pregnancy and surgery warnings.
  • Look it up yourself — a live PubMed reference link and a Wikipedia link on every card.

The Reference Library — lookup that stays live

  • Wikipedia, live — in-app search with summaries labeled as Wikipedia text and a link to the full article.
  • Live web research (optional) — you can add your own free API key in the app's Settings to turn on a broader web search. Results are clearly labeled as live web results, not nurse-reviewed, and each links to its source. The key is stored only in your own browser. The vendor ships no key and collects nothing.
  • From the Nurse's Library — cards cite the author's own books where they cover the topic, with the full library listed in the app.

The guide also includes a "Before taking any supplement" safety checklist and the free public sources it is built on (NIH Office of Dietary Supplements, MedlinePlus, NCCIH), so you can verify everything in it. Four of the 23 cards — Depression / anxiety, Chronic kidney disease, Cancer (during treatment), and Anyone on blood thinners — carry a clinician first note: talk to a clinician or pharmacist first, because supplements in those areas can interact with treatment.
